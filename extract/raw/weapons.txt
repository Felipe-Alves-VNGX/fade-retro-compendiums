A character's ability to use a particular type of weapon in
combat is represented by weapon feats. These give a
character increasing attack bonuses and access to special
abilities such as armour class bonuses as they get more
proficient with a weapon.

STARTING WEAPON FEATS
At 1st level, characters start with either two or four weapon
feats, which must be immediately spent on basic weapon
proficiency with different weapons usable by the character's
class. A character may not spend more than one 1st level
weapon feat on the same weapon.

GAINING WEAPON FEATS
As characters increase in level, they gain more weapon feats.
See CHAPTER 4 – CREATING A CHARACTER for further details.
These additional weapon feats cannot be spent immediately,
but must be spent while weapon training. See
CHAPTER 12 – GETTING BETTER for more information on weapon
training. By training, the character can spend these additional
weapon feats to either gain basic proficiency with new weapons
or increase their proficiency rank with existing weapons.

Spending a single weapon feat on a weapon will give the
character a single increase in proficiency rank with that
weapon (to a maximum rank of Grand Master proficiency)
regardless of how proficient the character currently is with
that weapon or how many other weapons the character is
proficient with.

There are 6 ranks of proficiency with each weapon:

■ Non-proficient
■ Basic proficiency
■ Skilled proficiency
■ Expert proficiency
■ Master proficiency
■ Grand Master proficiency

Characters start out non-proficient with all weapons other
than those they spent their starting weapon feats to gain
proficiency with.

   EFFECTS OF PROFICIENCY
   As a character gains more proficiency ranks with a weapon,
   they will usually do more damage with it. The damage done
   by a weapon is listed for each proficiency rank in the detailed
   weapon descriptions in this chapter.

   Some types of weapon have special abilities such as the
   ability to deflect attacks or the ability to ensnare opponents,
   which can be used only by characters with a high enough
   proficiency rank.

   OPPONENT TYPES
   Although some weapons are equally effective against any
   opponent, weapons intended for warfare are often more
   effective against other opponents armed with melee weapons;
   whereas weapons intended for hunting are often more effective
   against unarmed opponents. In the weapon descriptions in
   this chapter, each weapon has its effectiveness indicated
   against each type of opponent separately.

   Note that opponents wielding missile weapons or martial arts
   use the same values as opponents who are completely
   unarmed or using natural attacks such as claws.

   AMMUNITION & LOADING
   All missile weapons require ammunition, for example, bows
   need arrows and guns need bullets. In addition, guns also
   require a charge of red powder to work.

   Any character can load a weapon with ammunition, even those
   who are not proficient in its use. Loading a weapon does not
   take an action, and is assumed to be part of the normal attack
   action (see CHAPTER 11 – COMBAT for a full description of
   actions).

   If a character gets multiple attacks for any reason, they can
   reload between each attack. For example, a 28th level fighter
   is able to make three attacks against opponents that they
   can hit on a 2+ (after modifiers). That fighter could make all
   three attacks with a bow, crossbow, sling or gun and reload
   between each attack all in a single attack action.

   The only exception to this rule is with pistols. Although pistols
   require only one hand to fire, they require two hands to reload.

WEAPON SUMMARY
TABLE 6–1 shows a summary of the weapons that characters
can buy, as well as unarmed attacks (unarmed strikes and
wrestling) for comparison.

The columns in that table are as follows:

WEAPON
This is the name of the weapon. CHAPTER 9 – EQUIPPING FOR
ADVENTURE contains descriptions of each weapon listed here.

COST
This is the cost to buy one of these weapons. See
CHAPTER 9 – EQUIPPING FOR ADVENTURE for more information
about money and buying items. Note that none of the costs
for ranged weapons include ammunition, which must be
purchased separately.

BASE DAMAGE
This is the damage done by the weapon with basic proficiency.
Weapons do less damage if you are not proficient with them
and do more damage if your proficiency rank is above basic.

BASE TRAITS
These are the traits that the weapon has with basic proficiency.

Blunt: The weapon is blunt and does not have an edge.
Clerics may only use weapons that have this trait.

Bulky: The weapon is large and unwieldy. Thieves and
mountebanks may not use weapons that have this trait.

Charge: When the wielder of a weapon with the charge trait
is mounted and an attack is made after the wielder's mount
has charged 20' or more in the round, the weapon does double
the normal amount of damage.

Natural: The weapons is made from natural materials such
as wood, stone, and leather (or flesh in the case of unarmed
attacks). Druids may only use weapons that have this trait.

Off-hand: A weapon with this ability does not suffer the
normal to-hit and proficiency penalties when used in the
wielder's off hand. See CHAPTER 11 – COMBAT for more details
about wielding weapons in your off hand.

Simple: The weapon is easy to use and requires little
expertise. Wizards may only use weapons that have this trait.

Throw: Although primarily a melee weapon, this weapon may
also be thrown at opponents. Both melee and thrown attacks
with the weapon use the same proficiency.

Versatile: The weapon may be used either one-handed or
two-handed. The weapon has different statistics depending
on the number of hands used, but the same proficiency is
used for both one-handed and two-handed attacks.

ADVANCED TRAITS
Advanced traits are abilities that the weapon has when used
at a higher proficiency rank than basic. See Weapon Abilities
later in this chapter for when these abilities can be used and
what they do.

PROFICIENCY GROUPS
Weapons in the same proficiency group are used in a similar
manner, and when increasing your proficiency rank in one
weapon in a group you may also increase your proficiency
with others in the same group. See CHAPTER 12 – GETTING
BETTER for more details on increasing proficiency ranks.

SPECIAL WEAPON RULES
The following weapons are special cases that each require
their own rule:

■ Bastard Swords: Bastard swords use the same proficiency
  when used one-handed and two-handed.
■ Unarmed Strikes and Martial Arts: Martial arts has no
  proficiency of its own. When using martial arts, a mystic
  may use the attack bonus granted by their proficiency in
  unarmed strikes, but gets no other benefit from that
  proficiency.
■ Grenade-like Weapons: Throwing items that burst over
  an area such as flasks of oil, holy water, or red powder does
  not have a proficiency.

incoming attacks each round. For example, an entry of –2/–
2/–/– means that the wielder of the weapon gains a –2 bonus
to their armour class against two incoming attacks.

The character can decide which attacks to use the bonus
against so they can save the bonus for use against a tougher
opponent who is attacking later in the round rather than having
to use them against a weak opponent who happens to be
attacking first. However, the character must announce which
attacks they wish to use the bonus against before those
attacks are rolled.

These armour class bonuses must be used against attacks
coming from opponents of the relevant type, regardless of
what type of opponent the character is attacking.

For example, a character who is skilled with a normal sword
gets a –2 armour class bonus against one attack from an
armed opponent per round, even if the character is actually
attacking an unarmed opponent.

When a character uses two weapons in combat, they get
whichever AC bonus is most beneficial against each incoming
attack. The bonuses do not stack. To work this out, compare
the number of attacks each set of bonuses works against,
and pair them up until one weapon has run out of attacks that
it can give a bonus against. Each bonus can be used for either
value and attacker type in the pair.

Example: Black Leaf is wielding a normal sword in her main
hand and a buckler in her off hand. She has skilled
proficiency with the sword and basic proficiency with the
shield. The sword gives her an armour class bonus of –2/
–/–/– against attacks by an armed opponent. The shield
gives her –1/–1/–/– against attacks by any opponents.

Pairing these up, we get:

A first bonus that is –2 if the attack is armed (from the
sword) or –1 if the attack is either armed or unarmed (from
the shield).

A second bonus that is –1 if the attack is either armed or
unarmed (from the shield – there were no more sword
bonuses to pair up with this one).

Therefore, each round, Black Leaf has armour class bonuses
of –2/–1/–/– against armed attacks and –1/–1/–/– against
unarmed attacks.

In other words, she receives a –2 armour class bonus against
an armed attack (her first bonus) and a –1 armour class
bonus against any attack (her second bonus) or she can
receive a –1 armour class bonus against an unarmed attack
(her first bonus) and a –1 armour class bonus against any
attack (her second bonus).

In either case, the first bonus does not necessarily have to
be used against the first incoming attack. She can choose
which bonus is applied to which attack when the attacks
are declared as long as no bonus is used more than once.

Charge: When a weapon with the charge ability is used after
the wielder's mount has charged 20' or more in the round,
the weapon does double the normal amount of damage.

Deflect: If a number is listed for this ability, the wielder of
the weapon can attempt to deflect a number of incoming
attacks equal to this number by making a doom save against
each of them. A successful save means that the attack is
deflected and the character takes no damage.

The character can decide which attacks to try to deflect, so
they can save the attempts for use against a tougher
opponent who is attacking later in the round rather than having
to use them against a weak opponent who happens to be
attacking first. However, the character must announce which
attacks they wish to try to deflect against before attacks are
rolled.

When a character uses two weapons in combat, the deflection
attempts given by each weapon do not stack. The character
gets to use a number of attempts equal to the higher number
granted by either of the weapons.

Deflect Penalty: Weapons that have the deflect penalty
ability are hard to deflect because of their flexible nature. Any
attempts to deflect attacks with one of these weapons is
made at the listed penalty for the wielders proficiency rank.

Delay: When the wielder of a weapon with the delay ability
hits an opponent with it, the opponent must make a stasis
save or automatically lose initiative in the following round.

Skewer: If the weapon hits an opponent with the listed
number of hit dice (or levels) or less, the opponent may be
skewered if the attacker so chooses. The attacker must let
go of the weapon, and it remains caught in the opponent
for 1d4+4 rounds, doing 1d6 damage each round. If the
opponent spends a round removing the weapon, they take
double damage that round but are then free of the weapon.

Strangle: This works in an identical manner to the entangle
ability, with the addition that if the wielder of the weapon rolls
the number indicated or higher on the dice, before modifiers,
and the attack hits the target after modifiers, then the target
of the attack must make an immediate doom save with the
listed penalty or be paralysed and die after 1d6+2 rounds of
strangulation. Should the target be freed from strangulation,
they will remain paralysed for 2d6 rounds.

Stun: A weapon with the stun ability will stun any opponent
it hits that is approximately the same size as the wielder of
the weapon (or smaller) unless they make a doom save.

Some ranged weapons can only stun opponents at listed
ranges.

A stunned opponent cannot attack or cast spells and can only
move at ¾ normal speed. Stunned opponents also have a +2
penalty to armour class and a –2 penalty to all saves. A stunned
character can make a doom save each round to shake off the
stun.
