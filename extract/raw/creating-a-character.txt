      Chapter 4 – Cre

C  reating a player character for DARK DUNGEONS is a pretty
   straightforward process. All you will need is a pencil and
paper. You won't even need any dice.

STEP BY STEP PROCESS
STEP 1) NAME & CONCEPT
The first thing to do is to decide what sort of character you
want to play.

Do you want to be a brave warrior or a stealthy rogue? Do you
want to be human or non-human? Do you want to be dour or
happy-go-lucky? Are you a paragon of virtue or a conniving
schemer? It's important to decide what sort of character you
want to play before picking up any dice, but also important
to be flexible in such a concept and to talk to the Gameguide
first.

If the Gameguide is starting a game with 1st level characters
(which is the usual way to start a game) then deciding you
want to play an experienced swashbuckling pirate captain
who is an expert fencer isn't going to work. You would be
better off deciding you want to play someone who aspires to
be a swashbuckling pirate captain but is just starting out on
their adventuring career.

Similarly, if the Gameguide has decided that the campaign
will take place in a world where humans are the only intelligent
species, it's no good deciding that you want to play an elf or
a catboy.

When it comes to choosing an ancestry for your character,
you will need to ask the Gameguide what ancestries exist in
their world and are available for use as player characters.

If the Gameguide is using an existing published world then
this will probably have been decided for them, but if they have
invented their own setting for the game then it is up to them
what ancestries exist.

A few example ancestries are listed below for different types
of setting, but it's up to the Gameguide to decide which exist
in their setting. The setting might use one of the groups, more

  STEP 2) CHOOSE A CLASS
  Choose the class that you think best represents the sort of
  character you wish to play. While the classes represent
  archetypal characters, there is room for much variation within
  them.

  For example, a noble wishing to prove themselves worthy of
  knighthood, an "army brat" who grew up in a constantly moving
  mercenary camp, and an escaped slave trained for gladiator
  school would all be very different characters but they would
  all fit the "fighter" class.

STEP 4) FILL IN CLASS DETAILS
Now that you know what class your character is and what your
ability scores are, you can fill in your character sheet with the
abilities that your character gets from that class. Details of
these abilities are given in the description of each class.

ABILITIES COMMON TO ALL CLASSES
Hit Points: All characters have hit points as indicated for
their level in their class' ability table, where 'c' represents
their constitution modifier. At 1st level, for example, a
battlemage has "6+c"; so they will have 6 hit points plus a
number equal to their constitution modifier. At 2nd level, the
same battlemage will have "10+2c", i.e., 10 hit points plus a
number equal to twice their constitution modifier, and so
forth. Note that after 9th level characters no longer gain
additional hit points for their constitution modifier, but they
do still retain the ones they already have. If a character's
constitution modifier changes for any reason, their hit-points
should be retroactively adjusted as if their constitution had
always been this new value.

Attack Bonus: This is the base bonus that the character gets
when attacking in combat. The character will be adding either
their strength or dexterity modifiers to this, plus an additional
modifier based on their proficiency with the attack they are
using and possibly also another modifier if they are using a
magic weapon to make the attack. See CHAPTER 6 – WEAPONS
AND WEAPON PROFICIENCY and CHAPTER 11 – COMBAT for more
details on weapon feats and attacking.

Skills: All characters start with 4 skill points plus extra points
equal to their intelligence bonus. These points can be used
to improve the same skill or different skills. Characters gain
an additional skill point every four levels after level one (at 5th
level, 9th level, 13th level, etc.) and these additional skill points
can also be used to improve any skill. See CHAPTER 5 – ABILITY
CHECKS, SKILLS, & TALENTS for more details on how skills work.

Weapon Feats: Characters start with a number of weapon
feats, usually two but sometimes four, which must be spent
immediately on basic proficiency with different weapons. See
CHAPTER 6 – WEAPONS & WEAPON PROFICIENCY for a description
of how weapon feats work. Characters gain more weapon
feats as they rise in level (at 3rd level, 6th level, 9th level, etc.),
but these new weapon feats cannot be spent immediately
upon gaining the level – instead the character must undergo
additional training. See CHAPTER 12 – GETTING BETTER for
further details.

BATTLEMAGE ABILITIES (SEE TABLE 4–2A)
Equipment Restrictions: Battlemages may use any weapons
and armour.

Saves: Battlemages make saves using the bonuses listed on
Table 4–2b.

Spell Casting: Battlemages can cast wizard spells.
Additionally, a battlemage may (but does not have to) choose

to specialise in one of the four types of wizard spells: energy,
entropy, matter or spirit spells.

A battlemage who specialises in one of these types of spell
will find that type of spell easier to learn and prepare, but will
find spells of the opposite type more difficult to learn and
prepare. See CHAPTER 7 – SPELLS & SPELL CASTING for detailed
descriptions of these spells and specialisation.

Provided a battlemage has had a good night's sleep (8 hours),
they can spend an hour studying their spell book after waking
up in order to gain spell slots for the day as indicated on
TABLE 4–2A. A 1st level battlemage starts with only two 1st circle


--- page 29 ---
spells in their spell book, and must acquire more during their
adventures.

Some wizard spells are reversible. These spells can be
reversed in order to have an effect opposite to the normal
effect of the spell. A battlemage chooses whether or not to
reverse the spell at the time of preparation, not at the time
of casting. Battlemages may prepare any spell from their book
in either the normal or the reversed form (if the spell has a
reversed form), but may not prepare spells from someone
else's book or from a scroll; not even by using a Read Magic
spell.

Each prepared spell can be cast once during the day, and if
a battlemage wishes to cast a spell more than once then they
must prepare the spell more than once, taking up multiple
spell slots of the spell's circle.

A beginning battlemage starts with a spell book given to them
by their teacher or college, and this spell book will contain
the spell Read Magic and one other 1st circle spell of the
player's choice. This spell book is a gift from the character's
mentor and does not need to be paid for. See
CHAPTER 7 – SPELLS & SPELL CASTING for more information on
spells and spell casting.

Parry: At 7th level, a battlemage can choose to use the parry
action. The battlemage's player declares that their character
is parrying at the beginning of the round before initiative is
rolled. The battlemage makes no attacks during the round
(and therefore needs no initiative score), but all incoming
attacks are made at a –4 penalty.

Power Attack: At 11th level, a battlemage gains the ability to
make power attacks in melee. At the beginning of the round,
the battlemage's player declares that their character is
making a power attack. The battlemage automatically loses
initiative, and takes a –5 penalty to their attack roll for the
attack. However, if the attack hits then the battlemage gets
to add their strength score to the damage done by the attack
as well as their strength bonus. See CHAPTER 11 – COMBAT for
details of power attacks.

Multi-attack: At 14th level, a battlemage is able to make two
attacks per round against any creature that they are able to
hit by rolling a 2+ on the attack roll (after modifiers). At 26th
level, this rises to three attacks. See CHAPTER 11 – COMBAT for
details of multi-attack.

CLERIC ABILITIES (SEE TABLE 4–3A)
Equipment Restrictions: Clerics may wear any armour, but
may only use weapons with the Blunt trait.

Saves: Clerics make saves using the bonuses listed on
TABLE 4–3B.

Spells: Starting at 2nd level, clerics can cast clerical spells.
See CHAPTER 7 – SPELLS & SPELL CASTING for detailed
descriptions of these spells.

Provided a cleric has had a good night's sleep (8 hours), they
can spend an hour meditating and/or performing religious
rites after waking up in order to gain spell slots for the day
as indicated on TABLE 4–3A.

Every cleric has access to all cleric spells of circles they can
cast, and chooses freely which ones to prepare each day
within the limits of the numbers shown on TABLE 4–3A.

Each prepared spell can be cast once during the day, and if
a cleric wishes to cast a spell more than once then they must
prepare the spell more than once, taking up multiple spell
slots of the spell's circle. However, some spells will be easier
or more difficult to prepare than others of their circle based
on the domains that the cleric's patron immortal is aligned
with.

Some clerical spells are reversible. These spells can be
reversed in order to have an effect opposite to the normal
effect of the spell. A cleric chooses whether or not to reverse
the spell at the time of casting, not at the time of preparation.
Clerical spells are always prepared in their normal form.

See CHAPTER 7 – SPELLS & SPELL CASTING for more information
on spells and spell casting.

Turn Undead: clerics have the ability to channel divine power
in order to drive away or even destroy undead creatures such
as zombies or vampires.

When your cleric tries to turn undead, you must first decide
which undead you are targeting. If you are facing a mixed
group of undead you can only turn one type of undead each
round. Once you have decided which undead you wish to
attempt to turn, consult TABLE 4–3C and compare your cleric's
level with the type of undead that you are trying to turn. The
entry in the table will indicate the level of success, as follows:

'–': you are not powerful enough to turn this type of undead.

'+2': make a check with a bonus of +2. Your attempt at turning
the undead will be successful if the check succeeds. If the

Equipment Restrictions: Druids' distaste for manufactured
and refined goods means that wearing metal armour or using
metal weapons is anathema to their beliefs. They may only
use weapons and shields with the Organic trait, and may only
use leather armour.

Saves: Druids make saves using the bonuses listed on
TABLE 4–4B.

Spells: Starting at 2nd level, druids can cast druidic spells.
See CHAPTER 7 – SPELLS & SPELL CASTING for detailed
descriptions of these spells.

Provided a druid has had a good night's sleep (8 hours), they
can spend an hour meditating and/or performing religious
rites after waking up in order to gain spell slots for the day
as indicated on TABLE 4–4A.

Every druid has access to all druidic spells of circles they can
cast, and chooses freely which ones to prepare each day
within the limits of the numbers shown on TABLE 4–4A.

Each prepared spell can be cast once during the day, and if
a druid wishes to cast a spell more than once then they must
prepare the spell more than once, taking up multiple spell
slots of the spell's circle.

Some druidic spells are reversible. These spells can be
reversed in order to have an effect opposite to the normal
effect of the spell. A druid chooses whether or not to reverse
the spell at the time of casting, not at the time of preparation.
Druidic spells are always prepared in their normal form.

See CHAPTER 7 – SPELLS & SPELL CASTING for more information
on spells and spell casting.

insects or slugs are too simple for the ability to work on
them – although it does work on giant versions of these
creatures.

Commanded animals will obey a single mental command
from the druid. This command may be to flee, or to attack a
particular target, or to pick up an item and bring it to the

druid or any other command of a similar nature. All animals
included in a single command attempt must be given the
same command. The duration of the command lasts for 10
minutes or until the animals have finished the task that they
have been given, whichever happens first.

Mastered animals will become docile and friendly to the druid,
and if they are normally social or pack animals they will be
friendly to the druid's friends as well. They will fight to protect
the druid without being ordered to and willingly lay down their
lives on the druid's behalf.

At any time, the druid may (as a standard action) make mental
contact with any one of their mastered animals regardless of
distance (provided they are on the same plane of existence
and within the same celestial sphere). While in mental contact,
the druid experiences the animal's senses rather than their
own – so they see through the animal's eyes, hear through its
ears, and so forth – and can give the animal mental commands
at any time, or even physically take control of the animal's
movement.

The druid may not cast spells or use magical items through
the animal, and the druid's control of the animal's movement
is not fine enough for them to make the animal reproduce
human words.

Mastery of an animal is normally permanent, although a druid
can only have a total number of hit dice of animals mastered
at any given time equal to twice as many hit dice as the druid
has levels. A druid may relinquish mastery of an animal at
any time, and can do so at the same time as mastering a new
animal in order to make room for the new one.

Parry: At 7th level, a fighter can choose to use the parry action.
The fighter's player declares that their character is parrying
at the beginning of the round before initiative is rolled. The
fighter makes no attacks during the round (and therefore
needs no initiative score), but all incoming attacks are made
at a –4 penalty.

Multi-attack: At 10th level, a fighter is able to make two
attacks per round against any creature that they are able to
hit by rolling a 2+ on the attack roll (after modifiers). At 20th
level, this rises to three attacks and at 30th level it rises to

four attacks. See CHAPTER 11 – COMBAT for details of multiple
attacks.

Power Attack: At 11th level, a fighter gains the ability to make
power attacks in melee. At the beginning of the round, the
fighter's player declares that their character is making a
power attack. The fighter automatically loses initiative, and
takes a –5 penalty to their attack roll for the attack. However,
if the attack hits then the fighter gets to add their strength
score to the damage done by the attack as well as their
strength bonus. See CHAPTER 11 – COMBAT for details of power
attacks.

CHIVALRIC VOWS
After reaching 9th level, a fighter who has received a title of
nobility (knight or higher) might decide to take chivalric vows
and dedicate themselves to a cause such as a religious order
or a noble. Taking chivalric vows puts restrictions on the
fighter's behaviour but gives them extra abilities in exchange.
A fighter wishing to take chivalric vows must first find a suitable
chivalric order with which they share an outlook, and then
spend a month living with the order.

At the end of this time, the fighter undertakes a night long
vigil, and then becomes a chevalier. Once the fighter has
qualified in this way, the order places them with a particular
temple or noble who is a supporter of the order. The fighter
may or may not get a choice of liege, but betraying a liege is
considered betrayal of the order and doing so strips the
chevalier of all chivalric abilities and is also likely to incur the
wrath of the order.

All chevaliers must obey a strict code of conduct, the exact
details of which will vary from order to order but will usually
involve a requirement to provide hospitality and/or sanctuary
to fellow chevaliers of the same order. As a member of a
chivalric order, the fighter gains the following benefits:

Detect Evil: The chevalier can cast a Detect Evil spell (as per
the cleric spell of the same name) as often as they like.
Casting this spell does take the fighter's action for a round,
so cannot be done at the same time as attacking.

Spells: Chevaliers with a wisdom score of at least 9 can cast
cleric spells as if a cleric of one third the chevalier's level.
For example, a 17th level chevalier can cast spells as if a 6th
level cleric. All the normal rules and restrictions that apply to
a cleric's casting and preparation of spells also apply to the
chevalier.

Turn Undead: Chevaliers can turn undead as if a cleric of
one third the chevalier's level. For example, a 17th level
chevalier can turn undead as if a 6th level cleric. All the normal


--- page 40 ---
rules and restrictions that apply to a cleric's turning undead
also apply to the chevalier.

WARDENS
Certain chivalric orders pledge their allegiance to an area of
land rather than to a particular religion or noble. To these
orders, it doesn't matter who rules the land as long as the
land itself is healthy and free from monsters. Fighters who
join these orders must relinquish their title of nobility as part
of their investiture, and are known as wardens rather than
chevaliers.

Wardens have exactly the same benefits and strictures as
chevaliers, except that they cast druid spells rather than cleric
spells, and get the command animals ability (as if a druid of
one third their level) rather than turn undead.

WARLORDS
Fighters who choose to become neither chevaliers nor wardens
at 9th level become warlords instead. Unlike chevaliers or

wardens, a warlord need not pledge allegiance to any particular
cause. Instead they rely on their great experience and
expertise in strategy and tactics to become leaders of others.

Warlords are often found as the heads of mercenary companies
or armies. A warlord retains all of their existing abilities and
continues to rise in experience as normal, but also receives
the following additional benefits:

Encouragement: Once per round, during the statement of
intent phase, a warlord can exhort and encourage a single
ally within earshot, helping them to put aside pain and
exhaustion and keep fighting. The ally is immediately cured
of 1d6+1 damage. The ally must be able to understand the
warlord's words so it doesn't work on unconscious allies, on
allies who can't hear the warlord, or on allies who don't share
a common language with them.

A warlord can't encourage themselves, but two warlords can
encourage each other. Giving encouragement does not count
as an action for the warlord or the target. Each day, the warlord
can give encouragement a total number of times equal to
their level divided by three.

Tactics: At the beginning of each round, a warlord can assign
some or all of the benefits that they receive from weapon
feats with their current weapons (including their shield) to
one or more allies who are within earshot and who can
understand the warlord's words by a combination of advice,
warnings and orders. See CHAPTER 6 – WEAPONS & WEAPON
PROFICIENCY for more details about how the transferring of
weapon feat bonuses works.

Strategy: During mass battles, a warlord adds half their level
to the battle score of any army they command. See
CHAPTER 15 – WAR! for details.

Powder Refinement: Starting at 2nd level, grenadiers are able
to refine red powder into a more potent, but less stable, form.
At the start of each day, the grenadier can spend an hour
mixing some of the red powder that they own with other
substances in a carefully controlled manner to produce a
number of grains of refined powder based on their level as
listed on TABLE 4–6A. This refining process needs the
grenadier to be fresh of mind, and can not be done more than
once per day.

The amount of red powder required to produce the refined
powder is negligible, and the grenadier does not significantly
decrease the supply of powder they have on their person.
However, the grenadier does need to start with some red
powder – they can't just produce red powder from the air. Not
yet, anyway.

Refined red powder is less stable than normal red powder,
and it only lasts a day before decaying back to normal red
powder. Additionally, each grenadier has their own personal
method for refining the powder and incorporating it into
devices, and this means that no two grenadiers' refined
powders behave in exactly the same manner. Because of this,
one grenadier's refined powder can not be used by another
grenadier in their devices. Therefore, a grenadier only has as
much refined powder each day as they can make themselves
for that day.

Other than its potential use to power a grenadier's devices,
refined powder behaves in exactly the same way as normal
red powder. See CHAPTER 8 – RED POWDER for more
information about the properties of red powder.

As with thieves, they tend to be accepted by adventuring
parties more readily than by the general public, and their
charismatic nature often makes them the "face" of the party.
Although mountebanks are not the greatest fighters in the
world, their swashbuckling skills are not to be derided, and
their weak magic can actually serve as an asset. Because they
are unable to compete with battlemages or wizards in terms
of spells that do direct damage, it gives them the freedom

to prepare more utilitarian spells that more combat-magic
able spell casters would otherwise not have room for.

spell slots of the spell's circle.

reversed in order to have an effect opposite to the normal
effect of the spell. A mountebank chooses whether or not to

Equipment Restrictions: Mystics may use any weapon with
the Simple trait, but may not wear any armour.

Saves: Mystics make saves using the bonuses listed on
TABLE 4–8B.

Enhanced Martial Arts: Mystics use martial arts that are
enhanced by their mental abilities – enabling them to move
faster, hit harder, and dodge more successfully than other
unarmed combatants. The martial arts used by mystics is
distinct from other forms of unarmed combat such as brawling

and wrestling. It does not have proficiency ranks, instead
giving benefits per level as shown on TABLE 4–8A and
described individually below.

Martial arts uses both hands, so mystics cannot combine its
use with off-hand weapons. It can be done with a simple
weapon in hand, although the mystic does not gain the benefits
of their proficiency rank with the weapon when using it with
martial arts.

They either use the weapon normally and get all the benefits
of their proficiency with it (attack bonus, damage, deflect
attempts, etc.) or incorporate the weapon into their martial
arts – in which case the only benefit they get from the weapon
is its magical bonus to attack and damage rolls.

The choice of whether to make martial arts attacks, normal
unarmed attacks, or weapon attacks is made at the start of
each round during the Statement of Intent. See
CHAPTER 11 – COMBAT for more information on how rounds
work in combat.

class improves as shown on TABLE 4–8A. This armour class
applies whether using unarmed attacks or other weapons.

Move: The movement rate of mystics increases with level as
shown on TABLE 4–8A.

Unarmed Attacks: When using martial arts, a mystic is able
to make a number of attacks as shown on TABLE 4–8A. This
number of attacks only counts when using martial arts, and
not when using other weapons or other unarmed attacks such
as brawling or wrestling.

Unarmed Damage: When using martial arts, a mystic does
damage shown on TABLE 4–8A on successful attacks. This
damage is only done when using martial arts, not when using
other armed or unarmed attacks, although when using martial
arts the damage shown is done regardless of whether the
attack is done with or without a weapon.

Hit As: Starting at 2nd level, a mystic's martial arts attacks
are able to hit creatures that are only hit by silver weapons.
At 5th level, they are able to hit creatures only hit by +1
weapons. This increases to creatures only hit by +2 weapons
at 8th level, +3 weapons at 11th level, +4 weapons at 14th level,
and +5 weapons at 16th level. This ability only affects what
type of creatures the mystic is able to hit – it does not give
the mystic bonuses to hit opponents.

WIZARD ABILITIES (TABLE 4–11A)
Equipment Restrictions: Wizards are able to use any simple
weapon, but are not able to wear armour.

Spell Casting: Wizards can cast wizard spells. Additionally,
a wizard may (but does not have to) choose to specialise in
one of the four types of wizard spells: energy, entropy, matter
or spirit spells.

A wizard who specialises in one of these types of spell will
find that type of spell easier to learn and prepare, but will find
spells of the opposite type more difficult to learn and prepare.
See CHAPTER 7 – SPELLS & SPELL CASTING for detailed
descriptions of these spells.

Provided a wizard has had a good night's sleep (8 hours),
they can spend an hour studying their spell book after waking
up in order to gain spell slots for the day as indicated on
TABLE 4–11A. A 1st level wizard starts with only two 1st circle
spells in their spell book, and must acquire more during their
adventures.

Wizards may prepare any spell from their book in either the
normal or the reversed form (if the spell has a reversed form),
but may not prepare spells from someone else's book or from
a scroll; not even by using a Read Magic spell.

Each prepared spell can be cast once during the day, and if
a wizard wishes to cast a spell more than once then they must
prepare the spell more than once, taking up multiple spell
slots of the spell's circle.

Some wizard spells are reversible. These spells can be
reversed in order to have an effect opposite to the normal
effect of the spell. A wizard chooses whether or not to reverse
the spell at the time of preparation, not at the time of casting.

A beginning wizard starts with a spell book given to them by
their master, and this spell book will contain the spell Read
Magic and one other 1st circle spell of the player's choice. This
spell book is a gift from the character's teacher and does not
need to be paid for.

See CHAPTER 7 – SPELLS & SPELL CASTING for more information
on spells and spell casting.
